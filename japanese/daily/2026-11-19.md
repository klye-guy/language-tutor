# Daily lesson — 2026-11-19

Thursday is **stories and eating out day**. Video 1 is a classic fairy tale, **小さな赤いめんどり** (The Little Red Hen): a hen who keeps asking for help making bread and keeps hearing "no." Video 2 is a short drama about a Japanese-school student who calls an **居酒屋** (izakaya, a Japanese pub) to book a table for six, and keeps running into words he doesn't know.

Two new conversation tools today, both very useful:

- **誰か〜てくれる？** asks "can someone ~ for me?" (誰か手伝ってくれる？ = "Can somebody help me?"). The hen says it again and again, so you'll hear it many times.
- **〜ってなんですか？ / 〜ってどういう意味ですか？** means "What's ~? / What does ~ mean?" The student in Video 2 uses it every time he hits a new word, and the staff explain in easier Japanese. This is how you keep a conversation with a native speaker going when you get lost.

**Time:** about 14 minutes of video. Watch Video 2 twice (it's short), which brings it to about 20 minutes, plus exercises, a reading piece on an izakaya sign and menu, and two phrases to say out loud.

**How to watch:** Play each video once all the way through without stopping. The fairy tale repeats the same pattern four times, so it gets easier as it goes. For Video 2, the first time just follow what's happening. The second time, listen for the questions he asks.

Listen for words you already know coming back: パン, 作ります, 食べたい, おいしい, いただきます, 犬, 猫, ネズミ, 住んでいました, 毎日, 一緒に, 「…」と言いました, 〜ています, 金曜日, 〜時, 〜円, 〜人, おすすめ, 〜にします, いくらですか, 天気.

## Video 1: The Little Red Hen (7:40)

[The Little Red Hen | Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s5CMVlZgZok) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

She explains the word めんどり (hen), then tells the story with pictures. A hen lives with a dog, a cat, and a mouse. She finds a seed and decides to grow wheat and make bread. Every step of the way, she asks the others for help. At the end she connects the story to a Japanese saying.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| おんどり / めんどり | ondori / mendori | rooster / hen |
| むかしむかし / 昔々 | mukashi mukashi | once upon a time |
| たね / 種 | tane | seed |
| こむぎ / 小麦 | komugi | wheat |
| こむぎこ / 小麦粉 | komugiko | flour |
| はたけ / 畑 | hatake | field (for crops) |
| まきます / 蒔きます | makimasu | plant (seeds) |
| てつだいます / 手伝います | tetsudaimasu | help |
| だれか / 誰か | dareka | someone |
| だれも〜ません / 誰も〜ません | daremo ~masen | nobody ~ |
| ひとりで / 一人で | hitori de | by myself |
| しゅうかく / 収穫 | shūkaku | harvest |
| やきます / 焼きます | yakimasu | bake |
| いいにおい / いい匂い | ii nioi | smells good |
| ねむい / 眠い | nemui | sleepy |
| むり / 無理 | muri | no way, impossible |
| めんどくさい | mendokusai | what a pain, can't be bothered |
| おしまい | oshimai | the end |

### Exercises

**Comprehension** — English answers are fine.

1. What's the difference between おんどり and めんどり?
2. Who lived with the little red hen?
3. What did she find on the ground? What kind was it?
4. What was her plan? (There are four steps, from seed to bread.)
5. The first time she asked for help, what did the dog, the cat, and the mouse each say?
6. What did she do every day while the wheat grew?
7. How many times did she ask for help? Did anyone ever help?
8. What happened when the bread came out of the oven? What did the hen say, and what did she do?
9. What Japanese saying does the teacher mention at the end, and what does it mean?

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each. Copy the tone too: the animals sound lazy, the hen sounds fed up.

- ねえねえ、誰か手伝ってくれる？ (*Nē nē, dareka tetsudatte kureru?*) — "Hey, can somebody help me?"
- えー、やだ。手伝いたくない。 (*Ē, yada. Tetsudaitakunai.*) — "Ugh, no. I don't want to help."
- 眠いから無理。 (*Nemui kara muri.*) — "I'm sleepy, so no way."
- 誰も手伝ってくれません。 (*Daremo tetsudatte kuremasen.*) — "Nobody helps her."
- わかった。じゃあ、私一人でやるよ。 (*Wakatta. Jā, watashi hitori de yaru yo.*) — "Fine. Then I'll do it by myself."
- いい匂い！おいしそう！ (*Ii nioi! Oishisō!*) — "Smells good! Looks delicious!"

**Speaking — retell it.** Tell the story out loud in five or six sentences. Use these as a frame, and use 「…」と言いました for what the animals said.

1. 昔々、小さな赤いめんどりが ______ と住んでいました。
2. ある日、めんどりは ______ を見つけました。 (見つけました = found)
3. めんどりは「誰か ______ くれる？」と言いました。
4. 犬は「______」と言いました。猫は「______」と言いました。
5. 誰も ______ 。だから、めんどりは一人で ______ 。
6. 最後に、めんどりは ______ 。おしまい。

**Speaking — ask for help.** Ask an imaginary roommate for help with three things, then answer as the lazy roommate.

- 誰か ______ の、手伝ってくれる？ Try: カレーを作る / 買い物に行く / パンを焼く
- Lazy answers: えー、やだ。 / ______ から無理。 (眠い / 忙しい / 今テレビを見ている)
- Then the hen's answer: わかった。じゃあ一人でやるよ。

Note: 〜てくれる？ is casual, for friends and family. With a stranger or staff, make it polite: **手伝ってくれますか？**

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 1</summary>

1. おんどり is a male chicken (オスの鶏, rooster); めんどり is a female chicken (メスの鶏, hen).
2. A dog, a cat, and a mouse (犬と猫とネズミ).
3. A small seed (小さな種). It was a wheat seed (小麦の種).
4. Plant the seed in a field (畑に種を蒔く), grow wheat (小麦を育てる), turn the wheat into flour (小麦粉にする), and make bread with the flour (パンを作る).
5. Dog: 「えー、やだ。手伝いたくない。」 ("Ugh, no, I don't want to help.") Cat: 「眠いから無理。」 ("I'm sleepy, so no way.") Mouse: 「えー、めんどくさーい。」 ("Ugh, what a pain.")
6. She went to the field, watered it, and pulled weeds (水をあげたり草を抜いたりしました).
7. Four times: planting, harvesting (収穫), grinding the wheat into flour, and making the bread. Nobody ever helped (誰も手伝ってくれません).
8. It smelled great (とってもいい匂い). The dog, cat, and mouse came running: 「おいしそう！」「私も食べたい！」「僕にもちょうだい！」 The hen said 「このパンは私が作ったんだから、私が食べる。」 ("I made this bread, so I'm eating it."), said いただきます, and ate the whole thing herself. She gave none to the others.
9. 働かざる者食うべからず: "Those who don't work don't eat." 働かざる者 = people who don't work; 食う = 食べる; 食うべからず = must not eat.

## Video 2: Calling an izakaya to make a reservation (6:33)

[Calling an Izakaya to Make a Reservation | Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8FhnCK0q-3k) — Natural Japanese (NIJ)

A student is going out drinking with his Japanese-school classmates next Friday. Friday nights are busy, so he calls the izakaya to book. It's windy outside, the staff use words he doesn't know, and every time he asks what they mean. This is exactly what your first phone call in Japan will be like.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| いざかや / 居酒屋 | izakaya | Japanese pub |
| おさけ / お酒 | osake | alcohol, drinks |
| よやく / 予約 | yoyaku | reservation |
| でんわ / 電話 | denwa | phone call |
| らいしゅう / 来週 | raishū | next week |
| きんちょう / 緊張 | kinchō | nervous |
| かぜがつよい / 風が強い | kaze ga tsuyoi | it's windy |
| きこえます / 聞こえます | kikoemasu | can hear |
| もういちど / もう一度 | mō ichido | one more time |
| にちじ / 日時 | nichiji | date and time |
| なんめいさま / 何名様 | nanmei-sama | how many people? (polite) |
| コース | kōsu | set course meal |
| ねだん / 値段 | nedan | price |
| のみほうだい / 飲み放題 | nomihōdai | all-you-can-drink |
| せき / 席 | seki | seat |
| とうじつ / 当日 | tōjitsu | on the day |
| おくれます / 遅れます | okuremasu | be late |

### Exercises

**Comprehension**

1. Who is he going drinking with, and when? Is it his first time?
2. Why does he decide to call ahead?
3. What's the izakaya called?
4. What goes wrong at the start of the call? How does he fix it?
5. He doesn't know three words the staff use. What are they, and how does the staff explain each one?
6. What day and time does he book, and for how many people?
7. How much do the course meals cost? How much is 飲み放題, and for how long?
8. What does he finally choose, and why?
9. What happens if they're 15 minutes late?

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each. These are real phone lines you can reuse word for word.

- 忙しいところすみません。予約をしたいです。 (*Isogashii tokoro sumimasen. Yoyaku o shitai desu.*) — "Sorry to bother you when you're busy. I'd like to make a reservation."
- すみません、もう一度いいですか？ (*Sumimasen, mō ichido ii desu ka?*) — "Sorry, could you say that again?"
- すみません、日時ってなんですか？ (*Sumimasen, nichiji tte nan desu ka?*) — "Sorry, what's 'nichiji'?"
- 何名様ってどういう意味ですか？ (*Nanmei-sama tte dō iu imi desu ka?*) — "What does 'nanmei-sama' mean?"
- 来週の金曜日、夕方６時でお願いします。 (*Raishū no kin'yōbi, yūgata roku-ji de onegai shimasu.*) — "Next Friday at 6 p.m., please."
- 席だけ予約でお願いします。 (*Seki dake yoyaku de onegai shimasu.*) — "Just a table reservation, please."

**Speaking — make your own reservation.** Read the staff line, then answer out loud as yourself. Pick any day, time, and group size.

1. Staff: お電話ありがとうございます。居酒屋たぬきです。 → You: ______ 。予約をお願いします。
2. Staff: ご希望の日時を教えてください。 → You: ______ 曜日、______ 時でお願いします。
3. Staff: 何名様ですか？ → You: ______ 人です。
4. Staff: コースはどうしますか？ → Ask what it is: コースって ______ ？ Then ask the price: ______ ですか？
5. Then decide: ______ でお願いします。 (梅コース / 席だけ予約 / 飲み放題)
6. Staff: 15分遅れるとキャンセルになります。 → You: わかりました。よろしくお願いします。

**The lifesaver drill.** Say each of these words out loud as if you just heard it from a native speaker and don't know it. Use a different question each time: 〜ってなんですか？ / 〜ってどういう意味ですか？ / すみません、もう一度いいですか？ Words: お通し, 満席, ラストオーダー, 貸切.

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 2</summary>

1. Everyone from his Japanese-language school (日本語学校のみんな), next Friday (来週の金曜日). Yes, it's their first time going out for drinks together (初めてお酒を飲みに行きます).
2. Izakayas on Friday are really crowded (すごく人が多い / 混みそう).
3. 居酒屋CIJ.
4. It's windy (風が強い), so the staff can't hear him well (よく聞こえない). They ask もう一度いいですか？ He goes inside a room, and then they can hear him.
5. 日時: "when are you coming, what time are you coming" (いつ来ますか？ 何時に来ますか？). 何名様: "how many people are coming to the shop" (お店に来る人は何人ですか？). コース: a course meal where the food is already decided and you don't choose it yourself (料理が決まっています。自分で選びません).
6. Next Friday, 6 p.m. (夕方６時 / 18時), for six people (６人).
7. Three courses: 4,000, 5,000, and 6,000 yen. 飲み放題 is +1,500 yen for an hour and a half (１時間半). They recommend 飲み放題 if you take a course.
8. A seat-only reservation (席だけ予約). He wants to talk with his friends on the day about what to eat (何を食べたいか、友達と当日話したいです). They can still order 飲み放題 on the day.
9. The reservation is cancelled (キャンセルになります). The staff says to come on time (時間通りに来てください).

</details>

## Read it

Today's reading is an **izakaya sign and menu**, like the ones outside the door of a real one. Many izakayas show course prices as 松・竹・梅 (pine, bamboo, plum): 松 is the top tier and 梅 the basic one.

| Sign | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 居酒屋 | いざかや | izakaya, Japanese pub |
| 営業時間 | えいぎょうじかん | opening hours (Day 6) |
| 定休日 | ていきゅうび | regular closing day (Day 6) |
| ラストオーダー | rasuto ōdā | last order |
| 〜名様から | 〜めいさまから | from ~ people (minimum) |
| 飲み放題 / 食べ放題 | のみほうだい / たべほうだい | all-you-can-drink / all-you-can-eat |
| 生ビール | なまビール | draft beer |
| お通し | おとおし | small appetizer you're served (and charged for) automatically |
| 満席 | まんせき | full, no seats |
| 予約のお客様のみ | よやくのおきゃくさまのみ | reservations only |
| 税込 | ぜいこみ | tax included (Day 5) |

**The sign and menu**

```
【居酒屋　たぬき】
  営業時間　17:00〜24:00（ラストオーダー 23:30）
  定休日　月曜日

  ◆ コース料理（２名様から）
      梅コース　４,０００円
      竹コース　５,０００円
      松コース　６,０００円
  ◆ 飲み放題　１時間半　＋１,５００円
  ◆ 食べ放題　２時間　３,０００円
  ◆ 生ビール　５００円　／　ウーロン茶　３００円
  ※ お通し　お一人様　３００円
  ※ 価格はすべて税込です

  本日（金）　満席　予約のお客様のみ
```

1. What's the izakaya called? (Read the kana.) What time does it close, and when is last order?
2. Can you go on a Monday?
3. Which course is the most expensive? Which is the cheapest?
4. You're eating alone. Can you order a course?
5. Two of you order the 竹 course with 飲み放題. Count the お通し too. What's the total?
6. It's Friday and you walk in without a reservation. Can you get a table?
7. Do you pay extra tax?

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Read it</summary>

1. たぬき (Tanuki, a raccoon dog). Closes at 24:00 (midnight); last order is 23:30.
2. No, Monday is the regular closing day (定休日).
3. 松 is the most expensive (6,000 yen); 梅 is the cheapest (4,000 yen). The same three prices as in Video 2.
4. No: courses are for two people or more (２名様から).
5. (5,000 + 1,500) × 2 = 13,000 yen, plus お通し 300 × 2 = 600 yen, so **13,600 yen**. The お通し surprises a lot of visitors: it's a small dish you get without ordering, and it's on the bill.
6. No. Today (Friday) it's full (満席), reservations only (予約のお客様のみ). That's why the student in Video 2 called ahead.
7. No, all prices include tax (すべて税込).

_The sign and menu are a practice example made for this lesson, not a real shop._

</details>

## Use it today

Say these out loud at least once today.

1. **〜ってなんですか？ / 〜ってどういう意味ですか？** (*~tte nan desu ka? / ~tte dō iu imi desu ka?*) — "What's ~? / What does ~ mean?" Add **もう一度いいですか？** ("Could you say that again?") and you can get through a conversation even when you only understand half of it. Practice on three Japanese words you saw this week, like 押入れってなんですか？
2. **誰か手伝ってくれる？ / 手伝ってくれますか？** (*Dareka tetsudatte kureru? / Tetsudatte kuremasu ka?*) — "Can somebody help me? / Could you help me?" Ask someone at home in Japanese the next time you're cooking, even if they answer in English. If nobody does, use the hen's line: わかった、一人でやるよ。
