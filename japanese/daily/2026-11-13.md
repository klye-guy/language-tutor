# Daily lesson — 2026-11-13

Day four is **trains and stations**, the single most useful thing to handle on a trip to Japan. Video 1 is a picture talk about trains: how often you ride, how far the station is, ticket gates, platforms, and what people do on the train. Video 2 is a real walk through Fukuoka to Hakata Station, and Video 3 follows the teacher as she buys a bullet-train (新幹線) ticket at a machine and races to find her car before it leaves.

**Time:** about 22 minutes of video, plus exercises, a reading piece built from real station signs, and two phrases to say out loud. If you're short on time, do Videos 1 and 2 today and save Video 3 for the weekend.

**How to watch:** Play each video once all the way through without pausing to look things up. Follow the pictures and the street. Japanese subtitles are fine; skip English if you can. Video 3 is a little faster (it's labeled Beginner, not Complete Beginner), so it's today's stretch.

Same teacher as the last two days, so her voice will feel familiar. Listen for yesterday's words coming back: 都会, 田舎, 〜にします, and lots of 〜ました.

## Video 1: Trains and stations (7:38)

[Trains and Stations | Complete Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=42T37fExHy8) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

A picture talk: big stations and small stations, how she gets to hers, what you do once you're there, and what people do on the train.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| でんしゃ / 電車 | densha | train |
| えき / 駅 | eki | station |
| のります / 乗ります | norimasu | ride, get on |
| ちかい / 近い | chikai | near |
| とおい / 遠い | tōi | far |
| かいさつ / 改札 | kaisatsu | ticket gate |
| ホーム | hōmu | platform |
| きっぷ / 切符 | kippu | ticket |
| まちます / 待ちます | machimasu | wait |
| ねています / 寝ています | nete imasu | is sleeping |

### Exercises

**Comprehension** — English answers are fine.

1. How often does she ride the train?
2. Is the station near her house or far? How long does it take to walk there?
3. Big stations vs. small stations: name two things big stations have more of.
4. Her local station is small. How many ticket gates and how many platforms does it have?
5. Does she go to the station by bus, by car, or on foot? Why?
6. Once you get to the station, what are the steps until you're on the train? (Three steps.)
7. Name four things people do on the train.
8. What does she do on the train when she's alone? And when she's with her kids?

**Shadowing** — say each line out loud twice.

- 家から駅まで歩いて五分くらいです。 (*Ie kara eki made aruite go-fun kurai desu.*) — "From my house to the station is about five minutes on foot."
- 近いので歩いて行きます。 (*Chikai node aruite ikimasu.*) — "It's close, so I walk."
- 切符を買って改札を通ります。 (*Kippu o katte kaisatsu o tōrimasu.*) — "I buy a ticket and go through the gate."
- ホームで電車を待ちます。 (*Hōmu de densha o machimasu.*) — "I wait for the train on the platform."
- 電車で寝ている人もいます。 (*Densha de nete iru hito mo imasu.*) — "Some people sleep on the train."

**Speaking** — answer her questions about *you*, out loud, in full sentences.

1. 電車によく乗りますか？ — Use one of: 毎日乗ります / 週に〜回乗ります / あまり乗りません / たまに乗ります.
2. 家から駅は近いですか？遠いですか？ — 近いです / 遠いです. (No train station nearby? Swap in スーパー or 会社.)
3. ______ から ______ まで ______ 分くらいです。 (from where, to where, how many minutes)
4. ______ で行きます。 — 車で / バスで / 電車で, or 歩いて行きます.
5. 電車に乗ったら何をしますか？ — ______ を ______ ます。 (スマホを見ます, 音楽を聴きます, 本を読みます, 寝ます…)

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 1</summary>

1. Not often (あまり乗りません). Once every month or two (１ヶ月か２ヶ月に１回).
2. Near (近いです). About five minutes on foot (歩いて５分くらい).
3. Any two of: people (人), ticket gates (改札), platforms (ホーム), and trains (電車). Big stations are in the city (都会), small ones in the countryside (田舎).
4. Only four gates (改札は４つしかありません) and only two platforms (ホームも２つしかありません). しか…ありません means "only."
5. On foot. Not by bus, not by car, because it's close (近いので歩いて行きます).
6. Buy a ticket (切符を買います), go through the ticket gate (改札を通ります), then wait on the platform (ホームで電車を待ちます) and get on when it comes. She adds that these days more people use an IC card or a phone at the gate than a paper ticket.
7. Any four of: looking at phones (スマホを見ています), reading the newspaper (新聞), reading books (本を読んでいる), watching YouTube, listening to music (音楽を聴いている), sleeping (寝ている), and looking at the scenery outside (外の景色を見ている).
8. Alone: watches videos, listens to podcasts, or reads books. With her kids: talks with them and looks at the scenery.

</details>

## Video 2: Walking to Hakata Station (5:19)

[Walking to Hakata Station | Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2HmaxxGbiuk) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

She walks through downtown Fukuoka narrating what she sees: traffic lights, crosswalks, hotels, a taxi stand, and finally Hakata Station and the subway home. This is what Japan actually looks and sounds like on foot.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| あるいています / 歩いています | aruite imasu | am walking |
| しんごう / 信号 | shingō | traffic light |
| あか / 赤, あお / 青 | aka, ao | red, green ("blue") for traffic lights |
| おうだんほどう / 横断歩道 | ōdan hodō | crosswalk |
| わたります / 渡ります | watarimasu | cross |
| ちかてつ / 地下鉄 | chikatetsu | subway |
| ホテル | hoteru | hotel |
| タクシー | takushii | taxi |
| バスてい / バス停 | basu-tei | bus stop |
| おみやげ / お土産 | omiyage | souvenirs, gifts from a trip |

### Exercises

**Comprehension**

1. Where is she, and where is she walking to? What's special about that station?
2. What's the weather like?
3. The light turns from red to ... what? (Listen for the color word Japanese uses for a green light.)
4. What's at the bottom of the stairs that lead underground?
5. Name three kinds of buildings or places she passes on the way.
6. Who are all the people with suitcases? What languages does she hear?
7. What's inside the station besides trains? Name two things.
8. How does she go home at the end?

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each.

- 博多駅に向かって歩いています。 (*Hakata-eki ni mukatte aruite imasu.*) — "I'm walking toward Hakata Station."
- 青になったので横断歩道を渡ります。 (*Ao ni natta node ōdan hodō o watarimasu.*) — "It turned green, so I'll cross."
- 博多駅が見えてきました。 (*Hakata-eki ga miete kimashita.*) — "Hakata Station has come into view."
- 着きました。 (*Tsukimashita.*) — "We're here." (Great to say any time you arrive somewhere.)
- 地下鉄に乗って家に帰ります。 (*Chikatetsu ni notte ie ni kaerimasu.*) — "I'll take the subway home."

**Speaking — narrate your own walk.** Next time you're walking somewhere today (or just picture your street), describe it out loud like she does, one line at a time:

1. 今、______ にいます。 (where you are)
2. ______ に向かって歩いています。 (where you're heading)
3. ______ があります。 / ______ が見えてきました。 (two or three things you see: 信号, コンビニ, カフェ, ホテル, バス停…)
4. 着きました。

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 2</summary>

1. She's in Hakata, the center of Fukuoka (福岡の中心), walking to Hakata Station. It's the biggest station in Fukuoka (福岡で一番大きな駅).
2. Nice weather (今日は天気がいいですね).
3. 青 (*ao*). Japanese calls a green traffic light "blue."
4. An underground shopping street (地下街) that connects to the subway station (地下鉄の駅).
5. Any three of: office buildings (オフィスビル), cafés (カフェ), hotels (ホテル), a bus stop (バス停), a convenience store (コンビニ), a department store (デパート) next to the station, and the bus terminal (バスターミナル).
6. Travelers who came to Fukuoka on a trip (福岡に旅行に来ている人たち). She hears English, Korean, and Chinese.
7. Any two of: lots of souvenir shops (お土産屋さん), restaurants (レストラン), and a movie theater (映画館).
8. By subway (地下鉄). She goes down an escalator, buys a subway ticket, takes her change and ticket, and goes to the platform.

</details>

## Video 3: Taking the Shinkansen (8:55) — stretch

[Taking the Shinkansen | Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JT2Ro_TXaGg) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

She and her daughter take the bullet train from Hakata to Osaka. You see every screen of the ticket machine, which is exactly what you'd face on a trip. Don't worry about catching every word; follow the screens.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| しんかんせん / 新幹線 | shinkansen | bullet train |
| のりば / 乗り場 | noriba | boarding area, platform |
| きっぷうりば / 切符売り場 | kippu uriba | ticket office |
| けんばいき / 券売機 | kenbaiki | ticket machine |
| していせき / 指定席 | shitei-seki | reserved seat |
| じゆうせき / 自由席 | jiyū-seki | unreserved seat |
| かたみち / 片道 | katamichi | one way |
| おうふく / 往復 | ōfuku | round trip |
| 〜ごうしゃ / 〜号車 | ~gōsha | car number ~ |
| いそげ / 急げ | isoge | hurry! |

### Exercises

**Comprehension**

1. Where is she going, who with, and why?
2. What's the difference between 指定席 and 自由席? Which one does she choose, and why?
3. Which station do they get off at? Is it one way or round trip?
4. How does she pay, and how much is it?
5. One ticket says 小. What does that mean, and how much cheaper is it?
6. What's their train called, and which platform number is it?
7. What does she find at the shinkansen area before going up to the platform? Name two.
8. Which car (号車) are they in? What goes wrong on the platform?
9. What do she and her daughter do on the train?
10. Bonus: what two special shinkansen did she see at Hakata Station?

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each.

- 新幹線で大阪に行ってきます。 (*Shinkansen de Ōsaka ni itte kimasu.*) — "I'm off to Osaka on the shinkansen."
- 私は座りたいので、指定席にします。 (*Watashi wa suwaritai node, shitei-seki ni shimasu.*) — "I want to sit, so I'll go with a reserved seat."
- 新大阪で降ります。 (*Shin-Ōsaka de orimasu.*) — "I'm getting off at Shin-Osaka."
- 四号車はどこかな？ (*Yon-gōsha wa doko ka na?*) — "Where's car 4, I wonder?"
- よし、間に合った。 (*Yoshi, ma ni atta.*) — "Phew, made it!"

**Speaking — buy a ticket out loud.** You're at a station window. Say each line, filling in the blanks:

1. ______ まで、______ 枚お願いします。 (where to, how many tickets: 一枚 *ichi-mai*, 二枚 *ni-mai*)
2. 指定席 / 自由席 にします。
3. 片道 / 往復 でお願いします。
4. ______ で払います。 (カードで / 現金で)

Example: 大阪まで、二枚お願いします。指定席にします。往復でお願いします。カードで払います。

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 3</summary>

1. To Osaka, with her daughter, to see a K-pop idol concert (韓国のアイドルのライブ).
2. 指定席 can be reserved, so you're sure to get a seat; it costs a bit more. 自由席 can't be reserved, so you might not get a seat; it's cheaper. She picks 指定席 because she wants to sit (座りたいので、指定席にします).
3. Shin-Osaka (新大阪). One way for now (行きの切符のみ).
4. By credit card, not cash (現金じゃなくてクレジットカード). 23,720 yen (２万３７２０円).
5. 小 means child (子供). A child's fare is half price (半額).
6. Nozomi 24 (のぞみ２４号), the 11:15 departure bound for Tokyo. Platform 12 (１２番乗り場).
7. Any two of: souvenir shops (お土産屋さん), a convenience store (a FamilyMart, ファミマ), and Starbucks (スタバ). She also stops at the restroom (トイレ).
8. Car 4 (４号車). They head the wrong way (反対でした) while the train is about to leave, rush (急げ、急げ) past cars 7 and 5, and just make it (間に合った).
9. Her daughter eats snacks (お菓子) and then listens to music with headphones. She works until Osaka. The ride is about two and a half hours.
10. A One Piece shinkansen (アニメのワンピース) and a Hello Kitty one (キティちゃん).

</details>

## Read it

Today's reading is real station signage. You've already seen 入口 and 出口. Here's what the rest of a Japanese station looks like.

**Signs to know** — read each one out loud.

| Sign | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 駅 | えき | station |
| 改札 | かいさつ | ticket gates |
| 切符売り場 | きっぷうりば | ticket office |
| 乗り場 | のりば | boarding area |
| 〜番線 | 〜ばんせん | track / platform number ~ |
| 東口・西口・南口・北口 | ひがしぐち・にしぐち・みなみぐち・きたぐち | east / west / south / north exit |
| 地下鉄 | ちかてつ | subway |
| 新幹線 | しんかんせん | bullet train |

**A departure board** — this is the kind of board you'd see over a shinkansen platform.

```
時刻     列車名        行先      番線   自由席
11:15   のぞみ 24号   東京      12    1-3号車
11:23   さくら 548号  新大阪    13    1-3号車
11:36   みずほ 606号  新大阪    11    1-3号車
```

(時刻 *jikoku* = time, 列車名 *ressha-mei* = train name, 行先 *yukisaki* = destination.)

1. Which train is the teacher's from Video 3? Where is it going, and from which 番線?
2. Two trains on this board end their run at 新大阪. Which are they, and which platform does each leave from?
3. You have an unreserved (自由席) ticket. Which cars can you sit in?

**At the exit** — you're coming up from the platform and see this:

```
↑ 博多口       ← 筑紫口
↓ 地下鉄       → 新幹線 乗り換え
```

4. Which way to the subway?
5. Which way to transfer (乗り換え *norikae*) to the shinkansen?
6. 口 (*kuchi / guchi*) on its own means "mouth." Why does it show up in 出口, 入口, and 博多口?

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Read it</summary>

1. のぞみ 24号, going to 東京 (Tokyo), from track 12 (12番線). That matches the video: Nozomi 24, 11:15, bound for Tokyo, platform 12. She got off partway, at Shin-Osaka.
2. さくら 548号 (11:23, track 13) and みずほ 606号 (11:36, track 11). The 11:15 Nozomi also stops at Shin-Osaka, but it keeps going to Tokyo.
3. Cars 1 to 3 (1-3号車).
4. Down (↓ 地下鉄).
5. Right (→ 新幹線 乗り換え).
6. 口 means an opening or doorway, so it marks entrances and exits: 出口 is "out-mouth," 入口 is "in-mouth," and 博多口 is the Hakata-side exit.

_The departure board is a practice example made for this lesson, not a real timetable._

</details>

## Use it today

Say these out loud at least once today.

1. **着きました。** (*Tsukimashita.*) — "I'm here / we've arrived." Say it every time you get somewhere today: work, home, the store.
2. **〜まで、一枚お願いします。** (*~ made, ichi-mai onegai shimasu.*) — "One ticket to ~, please." Practice it with a few city names: 東京まで, 大阪まで, 京都まで.
