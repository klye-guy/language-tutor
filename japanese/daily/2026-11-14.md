# Daily lesson — 2026-11-14

Saturday is **food day**: eating out and snacking, the fun side of a trip to Japan. Video 1 is a short, very easy story about a monthly lunch date: where they meet, what they ate, and what they talked about. Video 2 is a taste test of five autumn snacks from a Japanese convenience store (コンビニ), with sweet potato and chestnut flavors everywhere. That's exactly what you'll find on the shelves if you visit in the fall.

## Video 1: Lunch with my mom (6:42)

[Lunch with My Mom | Complete Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S3w0xaOyvjg) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

A picture story: how often she has lunch with her mom, why they meet halfway, and what they ate at an Italian place.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| はは / 母 | haha | (my) mother |
| きのう / 昨日 | kinō | yesterday |
| ひるごはん / 昼ごはん | hirugohan | lunch |
| 〜かげつに１かい / 〜ヶ月に１回 | ~kagetsu ni ikkai | once every ~ months |
| あいます / 会います | aimasu | meet |
| まんなか / 真ん中 | mannaka | the middle, halfway |
| くるま / 車 | kuruma | car |
| おみせ / お店 | o-mise | shop, restaurant |
| こうちゃ / 紅茶 | kōcha | (black) tea |
| さとう / 砂糖 | satō | sugar |
| しゃべりました / 喋りました | shaberimashita | chatted |
| たのしかったです / 楽しかったです | tanoshikatta desu | it was fun |

### Exercises

**Comprehension** — English answers are fine.

1. Who did she have lunch with, and when?
2. How often do they have lunch together?
3. Are their houses close together or far apart? How long is the drive?
4. Where do they always meet, and why? How far is it from each house?
5. What kind of restaurant did they go to? What was in her pasta? (Three things.)
6. What did she have for dessert?
7. Did she drink coffee? What did she drink, and what did she put in it?
8. Name two or three things they talked about. How did she feel about the lunch?

**Shadowing** — say each line out loud twice.

- 昨日、母と昼ごはんを食べました。 (*Kinō, haha to hirugohan o tabemashita.*) — "Yesterday I had lunch with my mom."
- 一ヶ月に一回、母と会って、ランチをします。 (*Ikkagetsu ni ikkai, haha to atte, ranchi o shimasu.*) — "Once a month, I meet my mom and we have lunch."
- 車で一時間半くらいです。 (*Kuruma de ichi-jikan han kurai desu.*) — "It's about an hour and a half by car."
- パスタもティラミスもおいしかったです。 (*Pasuta mo tiramisu mo oishikatta desu.*) — "Both the pasta and the tiramisu were delicious."
- 食べながら、たくさん喋りました。 (*Tabe nagara, takusan shaberimashita.*) — "We talked a lot while we ate."

**Speaking** — tell your own meal story, out loud, in full sentences. Pick a real meal from this week.

1. ______ 、______ と ______ を食べました。 (when, who with, what: 昨日、友達とピザを食べました。 Alone? 一人で *hitori de*.)
2. ______ に一回、______ と会います。 (how often, who: 一週間に一回 / 一ヶ月に一回)
3. ______ に行きました。 (what kind of place: イタリアンのお店, ラーメン屋, カフェ…)
4. ______ も ______ もおいしかったです。 (two things you ate or drank)
5. 楽しかったです。 / おいしかったです。 — end on how it was.

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 1</summary>

1. Her mother (私の母 / お母さん), yesterday (昨日).
2. Once a month (１ヶ月に１回).
3. A bit far (ちょっと遠い; 近くないです). About an hour and a half by car (車で１時間半くらい).
4. In the middle, halfway between (いつも真ん中で会います), because the houses are far apart. It's 45 minutes from her house and 45 minutes from her mom's.
5. An Italian place (イタリアンのお店). Tomato, bacon, and mushroom pasta (トマトとベーコンとキノコのパスタ).
6. Tiramisu (ティラミス). Both the pasta and the tiramisu were delicious.
7. No coffee (コーヒーじゃないです). Black tea (紅茶) with sugar and milk (砂糖とミルク), so milk tea (ミルクティー). That was delicious too.
8. Any of: the kids (子供のこと), work (仕事のこと), and travel (旅行のこと). It was fun (楽しかったです), and she's looking forward to next month (来月も楽しみです).

</details>

## Video 2: Convenience store fall snacks (11:49) — stretch

[Japanese Convenience Store Fall Snacks | Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EU3S99kWAiI) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

She buys five autumn snacks at the konbini, opens each one, smells it, tastes it, and compares. Watch the packages: she reads the flavor names off them, which feeds straight into today's reading.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| あき / 秋 | aki | autumn, fall |
| おかし / お菓子 | o-kashi | snacks, sweets |
| 〜あじ / 〜味 | ~aji | ~ flavor |
| さつまいも | satsumaimo | Japanese sweet potato |
| くり / 栗 | kuri | chestnut |
| ひとくち / 一口 | hitokuchi | one bite |
| 〜てみます | ~te mimasu | try doing ~ (食べてみます) |
| におい / 匂い | nioi | smell |
| かわいい / 可愛い | kawaii | cute |
| どうぶつ / 動物 | dōbutsu | animal |
| りょうほう / 両方 | ryōhō | both |
| 〜のほうがすき / 〜の方が好き | ~ no hō ga suki | like ~ better |

### Exercises

**Comprehension**

1. Where did she just go, and what did she buy there? How many?
2. What season is it? What is she still wearing, even so?
3. Snack 1 is a チョコパイ. What flavor is it, and where is the flavor?
4. Why does she only take one bite of the first snack?
5. Snack 2 is a small chocolate. What's it called, what flavor is it, and what's cute about it? What will she do with the rest?
6. Snacks 3 and 4 are the same brand of biscuit. What's special about their shape? Name two animals she gets.
7. What does おさつ mean? What's the 4th snack's flavor, and what is a モンブラン?
8. Sweet potato vs. chestnut: which did she like better?
9. Before she even opens snack 5, what does she notice? What does 和栗 mean?
10. Besides snacks, what other sweet potato and chestnut foods show up in Japan in the fall?

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each.

- コンビニでお菓子を買ってきました。 (*Konbini de o-kashi o katte kimashita.*) — "I went to the konbini and bought some snacks."
- 一口食べてみます。 (*Hitokuchi tabete mimasu.*) — "I'll try a bite."
- さつまいもの味がする。 (*Satsumaimo no aji ga suru.*) — "It tastes like sweet potato."
- 両方おいしいです。 (*Ryōhō oishii desu.*) — "They're both good."
- 私はさつまいもの方が好きでした。 (*Watashi wa satsumaimo no hō ga suki deshita.*) — "I liked the sweet potato one better."

**Speaking — run your own taste test.** Grab any two snacks or drinks you have at home today. Say each line out loud as you go, like she does:

1. これは ______ です。 ______ 味です。 (what it is, what flavor: チョコ味, いちご味, バニラ味…)
2. 一口食べてみます。 / 一口飲んでみます。
3. ______ の味がします。 / ______ の匂いがします。 (what it tastes or smells like)
4. ______ と ______ 、どっちが好きですか？ (ask yourself which you like better)
5. ______ の方が好きです。 or 両方おいしいです。

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 2</summary>

1. The convenience store (コンビニ). Snacks (お菓子): five of them (５個).
2. Autumn (秋). She's still wearing short sleeves (まだ半袖を着てますけど).
3. Sweet potato (さつまいも味). The cream inside is sweet-potato flavored (中のクリームが、さつまいも味です).
4. She wants to eat it all, but she still has lots of snacks to try (まだいっぱいお菓子があるので、これは一口だけにします).
5. チロルチョコ (Tirol Choco), sweet potato (スイートポテト) flavor. The package itself looks like a sweet potato (パッケージもスイートポテトみたい), and there's a picture of a sweet potato on the back. Five small pieces came inside; she eats one and saves the other four to give to her kids later (後で子供たちにあげます).
6. They're shaped like animals (動物の形のビスケット), and the brand name is たべっ子どうぶつ. Any two of: a dog (犬), a sparrow (スズメ), a rhinoceros (サイ), and a chicken (鶏).
7. おさつ means sweet potato (さつまいものこと); snack 3 is おさつバター, sweet potato and butter. Snack 4 is モンブラン風味 (Mont Blanc flavor); a モンブラン is a chestnut cake (栗のケーキ), and she loves it (大好き).
8. Both were good (どっちも美味しい / 両方美味しい), but she liked the sweet potato one better (さつまいもの方が好きでした). After three snacks, the おさつバター biscuit was her favorite so far (今のところ、これが一番好きです).
9. It already smells like chestnut before it's open (まだ開けてないのに、もう栗の匂いがします). 和栗 means Japanese chestnuts (日本の栗). The snack is called アルフォート (Alfort), a chocolate-and-biscuit snack, and it has a stronger chestnut taste than the biscuit.
10. Bread (パン), ice cream (アイス), and other sweets (スイーツ). Her advice: if you come to Japan in the fall, go look for them (ぜひ探してみてください).

</details>

## Read it

Today's reading is **snack and food labels**, the stuff you'll squint at in a konbini aisle. Two new kanji do a lot of the work: 味 (*aji*, flavor) and 限定 (*gentei*, limited).

**Words to know** — read each one out loud.

| Label | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 〜味 | 〜あじ | ~ flavor |
| さつまいも | さつまいも | sweet potato (usually in hiragana on labels) |
| 栗 | くり | chestnut |
| 和栗 | わぐり | Japanese chestnut |
| 秋限定 | あきげんてい | autumn only, fall limited edition |
| 期間限定 | きかんげんてい | limited time only |
| 新発売 | しんはつばい | new release |
| 税込 | ぜいこみ | tax included |
| 抹茶 | まっちゃ | matcha (green tea) |

**The snack shelf** — imagine this row of price tags at a konbini.

```
① 【秋限定】 さつまいも味チョコパイ        198円（税込）
② 【新発売】 和栗モンブラン味ビスケット    158円（税込）
③ 【期間限定】 抹茶味アイス               240円（税込）
④ しお味ポテトチップス                    148円（税込）
```

1. Which snack is sweet potato flavor? Is it on the shelf all year?
2. Which one is brand new? What flavor is it?
3. You want something cold. Which number, what flavor, and how much?
4. Which one is a normal, every-day item? What flavor? (Hint: you saw this word on Day 2 with ramen.)
5. You buy ① and ②. How much do you pay? Do you need to add tax?
6. 和 shows up in 和栗. You may also see 和食 (*washoku*) on restaurant signs. Guess what 和食 means.

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Read it</summary>

1. ① さつまいも味チョコパイ. No, it says 秋限定: autumn only.
2. ② (新発売). Japanese chestnut Mont Blanc flavor (和栗モンブラン味), like the last snack in Video 2.
3. ③ 抹茶味アイス, matcha flavor, 240 yen.
4. ④ しお味ポテトチップス, salt flavor (しお = 塩, as in 塩ラーメン). It has no 限定 or 新発売 tag.
5. 198 + 158 = 356 yen. No extra tax: 税込 means tax is already included.
6. Japanese food. 和 means "Japanese" (和栗 = Japanese chestnut, 和食 = Japanese cuisine).

_The shelf tags are a practice example made for this lesson, not real prices._

</details>
