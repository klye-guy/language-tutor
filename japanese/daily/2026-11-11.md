# Daily lesson — 2026-11-11

Day two is about **introducing yourself** and **food you can actually get in Japan**: ramen and a late-night convenience store (コンビニ, *konbini*) run. All three videos are spoken in Japanese with pictures or real footage, and they recycle yesterday's 食べます, 飲みます, 好き, いただきます, and おいしい.

**Time:** about 19 minutes of video, plus exercises, a short reading piece, and two phrases to say out loud. It should fit in about 30 minutes.

**How to watch:** Play each video once all the way through without pausing to look things up. Follow the pictures. Japanese subtitles are fine; skip English if you can. Video 3 has English subtitles burned in, so try covering the bottom of the screen, or just don't read them on the first watch.

Video 1 is the most useful one for conversation: it's built from exactly the questions people will ask you when you meet them. Video 3 is a bit faster and more natural, so it's the stretch for today.

## Video 1: My self-introduction (6:34)

[My Self Introduction | Complete Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T_5sjgI83kc) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

Yuki introduces herself (name, age, nationality, where she lives, her job), and then asks "is it this or that?" about each fact so you hear every sentence many times.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| じこしょうかい / 自己紹介 | jikoshōkai | self-introduction |
| なまえ / 名前 | namae | name |
| 〜さい / 〜歳 | ~sai | ~ years old |
| にほんじん / 日本人 | nihonjin | Japanese person |
| アメリカじん / アメリカ人 | amerikajin | American |
| すんでいます / 住んでいます | sunde imasu | live (in a place) |
| せんせい / 先生 | sensei | teacher |
| ちがいます / 違います | chigaimasu | that's not right / no |

### Exercises

**Comprehension** — English answers are fine.

1. What is her name, and how old is she?
2. Which city does she live in? Which city does she say she does *not* live in?
3. What is her job? Name one job she says she does *not* have.
4. What does she make?
5. At the end she asks you five questions. What are they about?

**Shadowing** — say each line out loud twice.

- 私の名前はゆきです。 (*Watashi no namae wa Yuki desu.*)
- 私はアメリカ人ではありません。 (*Watashi wa amerikajin dewa arimasen.*) — "I'm not American."
- 東京ではなくて福岡に住んでいます。 (*Tōkyō dewa nakute Fukuoka ni sunde imasu.*) — "I live in Fukuoka, not Tokyo."
- お名前は何ですか？ (*O-namae wa nan desu ka?*) — "What's your name?"

**Speaking** — answer Yuki's five questions out loud, about you. One short sentence each is perfect.

1. お名前は何ですか？ — 私の名前は ______ です。
2. 何歳ですか？ — ______ 歳です。
3. どこの国の方ですか？ — ______ 人です。
4. どこに住んでいますか？ — ______ に住んでいます。
5. お仕事は何ですか？ — ______ です。 (If your job is hard to say, 会社員 *kaishain*, "office worker," works for most jobs.)

Then say all five in a row as one self-introduction, and finish with **よろしくおねがいします** (*yoroshiku onegai shimasu*, "nice to meet you").

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 1</summary>

1. Yuki (ゆき), 35 years old (35歳). She's not 25 or 45.
2. Fukuoka (福岡), in Japan. Not Tokyo (東京ではなくて福岡). She also asks "Osaka?" and the answer is still Fukuoka.
3. A Japanese teacher (日本語の先生). She's not a nurse (看護師) and not a programmer (プログラマー).
4. Japanese videos (日本語の動画). Not songs (歌) and not books (本).
5. Your name, your age, which country you're from, where you live, and your job.

</details>

## Video 2: Ramen (5:25)

[Ramen | Beginner Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iLLU6J_H0UU) — にほんごのじかん | Japanese Comprehensible Input (CI)

The same teacher talks about her favorite noodle dish: how often she eats ramen, the main soup types, and which city is famous for which kind. Handy for ordering in Japan.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| ラーメン | rāmen | ramen |
| めん / 麺 | men | noodles |
| いちばん / 一番 | ichiban | the most / number one |
| だいすき / 大好き | daisuki | love, like a lot |
| しょうゆ / 醤油 | shōyu | soy sauce |
| みそ / 味噌 | miso | miso |
| しお / 塩 | shio | salt |
| とんこつ / 豚骨 | tonkotsu | pork bone (broth) |
| ゆうめい / 有名 | yūmei | famous |

### Exercises

**Comprehension**

1. Name two other noodle dishes she mentions besides ramen.
2. About how many times a month does she go to a ramen shop?
3. What kind of ramen does she eat at home? At which meal does she eat it, and at which meal does she *not*?
4. Name the four ramen types. What makes them different?
5. Match the place to its famous ramen: Sapporo, Hakodate, Tokyo, Fukuoka.

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each.

- 私はラーメンが一番好きです。 (*Watashi wa rāmen ga ichiban suki desu.*)
- 私はラーメンが大好きです。 (*Watashi wa rāmen ga daisuki desu.*)
- 東京は醤油ラーメンが有名です。 (*Tōkyō wa shōyu rāmen ga yūmei desu.*)
- お昼ごはんによく食べます。 (*O-hirugohan ni yoku tabemasu.*) — "I often eat it for lunch."

**Speaking** — out loud, about you.

- 私は ______ ラーメンが一番好きです。 (Pick one: 醤油, 味噌, 塩, 豚骨. If you haven't tried them, say which you want to try: ______ ラーメンが食べたいです。)
- Now name your own favorite food: 私は ______ が大好きです。
- And one more: ______ によく ______ を食べます。 (e.g. 朝ごはんによくパンを食べます。)

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 2</summary>

1. Udon (うどん) and soba (そば).
2. About three times a month (１ヶ月に３回くらい).
3. Cup ramen and bagged instant ramen (カップラーメン, インスタントラーメン). She often eats it for lunch (お昼ごはん), never for breakfast (朝ごはんには食べません).
4. Shōyu (soy sauce), miso, shio (salt), and tonkotsu (pork bone). The soup is different (スープが違います).
5. Sapporo is miso, Hakodate is shio (salt), Tokyo is shōyu (soy sauce), and Fukuoka is tonkotsu. She lives in Fukuoka and always eats tonkotsu.

</details>

## Video 3: Midnight konbini (6:55)

[Comprehensible Japanese - Midnight Konbini / Japanese Listening Practice](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cNDzCgsTeyg) — Nihongo-Learning

Yuta (from yesterday's morning-routine video) gets hungry at night, walks to the convenience store, and eats what he bought on camera. Real Japanese street and food footage. The last minute or so is an ad for a learning app, which you can skip.

### Before you watch

| Japanese | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| コンビニ | konbini | convenience store |
| よる / 夜 | yoru | night |
| おなかがすきました / お腹が空きました | onaka ga sukimashita | I'm hungry |
| かいました / 買いました | kaimashita | bought |
| おにぎり | onigiri | rice ball |
| みそしる / みそ汁 | misoshiru | miso soup |
| おゆ / お湯 | oyu | hot water |
| はし | hashi | chopsticks |
| さむい / 寒い | samui | cold (weather) |

### Exercises

**Comprehension**

1. What time is it at the start of the video?
2. Why does he go to the convenience store?
3. How many things does he buy, and what are they?
4. What's inside his onigiri?
5. How long are Japanese convenience stores open?

**Shadowing** — out loud, twice each.

- ちょっとお腹が空きました。 (*Chotto onaka ga sukimashita.*) — "I'm a little hungry."
- コンビニに行きましょう。 (*Konbini ni ikimashō.*) — "Let's go to the konbini."
- 2つのものを買いました。 (*Futatsu no mono o kaimashita.*) — "I bought two things."
- 夜に飲むみそ汁は、とってもおいしいですね。 (*Yoru ni nomu misoshiru wa, tottemo oishii desu ne.*)

**Speaking** — imagine you just got back from a konbini in Japan. Say out loud what you bought and what you'll eat or drink.

- ______ と ______ を買いました。
- まずは ______ を食べます。 / ______ を飲みます。

Example: おにぎりとコーヒーを買いました。まずはおにぎりを食べます。いただきます！

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Video 3</summary>

1. 9:49 at night (夜の9時49分).
2. He's a little hungry (ちょっとお腹が空きました), and he wants to buy some food.
3. Two things (2つ): an onigiri and miso soup. The onigiri is ume (pickled plum) and the miso soup has green onion (長ネギ).
4. Umeboshi, a pickled plum (梅干し). He says he likes umeboshi.
5. 24 hours a day (24時間), any time: morning, noon, or night.

</details>

## Read it

Today's reading is the kind of thing you'd see at a ramen shop's ticket machine and on a konbini door. Read each one out loud, sounding it out in kana first.

**Katakana and hiragana from today's videos**

1. ラーメン
2. コンビニ
3. おにぎり
4. みそしる
5. とんこつ

**A ramen ticket machine**

Many ramen shops have you buy a ticket from a machine before you sit down. Imagine these buttons:

```
しょうゆラーメン   800円
みそラーメン       900円
しおラーメン       850円
とんこつラーメン   950円
```

6. Which ramen is the cheapest, and which costs the most?
7. You want the soy-sauce one. Which button do you press?

**Two survival signs** (shop doors and windows)

| Sign | Reading | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 24時間 | にじゅうよじかん (*nijūyo-jikan*) | 24 hours (open all day and night) |
| 営業中 | えいぎょうちゅう (*eigyō-chū*) | open for business |

8. A shop door says 営業中. Can you go in?
9. You need food at 3 AM. What sign on a konbini tells you it's open?

<details>
<summary>Answer key — Read it</summary>

1. *rāmen* — ramen
2. *konbini* — convenience store
3. *onigiri* — rice ball
4. *misoshiru* — miso soup
5. *tonkotsu* — pork-bone (broth)
6. しょうゆラーメン is cheapest (800円); とんこつラーメン costs the most (950円).
7. しょうゆラーメン.
8. Yes, it means the shop is open.
9. 24時間.

</details>
