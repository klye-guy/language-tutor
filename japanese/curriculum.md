# Japanese curriculum

_Draft. Will be finalized after Kyle's starting level is known. If Kyle provides his own curriculum, it replaces this._

## Approach
- **Comprehensible input first:** lots of listening to video that's mostly understandable, with visual support.
- **Use it right away:** each lesson ends with speaking or shadowing tasks so input turns into output.
- **Spaced recycling:** vocab and grammar from earlier lessons reappear in later ones.

## Planned stages
1. **Foundations:** sounds of Japanese, hiragana and katakana, greetings and survival phrases, super-beginner CI.
2. **Beginner conversation:** self-introduction, daily life, basic verbs and particles, polite (desu/masu) form.
3. **Everyday conversation:** casual speech, past and te-form, asking and answering questions, storytelling.
4. **Intermediate:** opinions, plans, keigo awareness, native-speed content with support.

## Reading track
- Woven into the daily lessons (the **Read it** section), not treated as a separate course.
- **First:** kana fluency (hiragana and katakana), using words from that day's videos.
- **Then:** practical survival kanji and signage — entrances and exits, station names, menu items, prices, and restroom signs.
